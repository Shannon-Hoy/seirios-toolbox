Trip & legs

Build the trip as ordered legs: layovers, shore stays, and time underway. Dates run consecutively from the start date. The first and last days of the whole trip are treated as travel days at 75% M&IE (FTR §301-11.101); toggle any day below.

Underway incidentals:
Modes. Shore, hotel pays lodging plus the leg's M&IE. Shore, lodging aboard pays full M&IE with no lodging (in port, sleeping on the ship). En route, in transit is for multi-day flights and connections: no lodging, full M&IE, nothing auto-deducted, since airline meals never reduce per diem; use the M&IE rate of the locality where you next sleep. Underway, meals & quarters aboard deducts all three meals as Government/contract-provided, leaving incidentals only, which covers tips to ship stewards under the FTR definition (§300-3.1); zero the Incid. column if your client agreement pays nothing while embarked.
Rates. The M&IE dropdown carries the FY2026 GSA CONUS tiers. For OCONUS legs (Hawaii and territories via DTMO, foreign via State Dept), choose Custom and enter the locality's M&IE total, or just type the post into the Location field. The meal split follows the official DSSR breakdown (breakfast 15%, lunch 25%, dinner 40%, rounded to whole dollars, incidentals as the ~20% remainder, per allowances.state.gov); the Incid. column overrides the remainder when your client agreement pays a different amount aboard. On a travel day, the rate used should be the locality where you spend that night.
Date line. Mark the leg on which you cross the international date line. Westbound (toward Fiji) skips one calendar date before that leg's last day, so the paid day count stays honest while the dates match your tickets. Eastbound repeats the previous date on the leg's last day, flagged in the grid, and both occurrences are paid as separate days in travel status.
Meal deductions. Tick a meal on a day below when it is provided by the Government or included in a registration fee; its value is deducted from that day's M&IE, never below incidentals (FTR §301-11.18). Complimentary hotel breakfasts and airline meals do not reduce per diem (FTR §301-11.17), so only tick breakfast when it is genuinely provided under the event or contract, or when your client agreement says otherwise.

Type a location straight into a leg and the per diem fills itself: a foreign post from the built-in State Dept table, or City, ST from the built-in GSA master file, both offline; unlisted CONUS locations correctly fall back to the standard rate, or type Standard CONUS yourself. A 5-digit ZIP uses the GSA API (needs the key below). Alaska, Hawaii and territories have no public rate file or API; look them up at the DTMO site and enter the numbers on the leg.

Update either table: drop the monthly foreign Excel from allowances.state.gov or the annual GSA Per Diem Master Rates File from the GSA per diem files page here (CSV exports work too); the tool recognises which is which. Updates are cached in this browser, so the app file itself never grows; the built-in snapshots stay as the fallback.
Rate watch calendar. This tool checks its own tables every time it opens, but the sources publish on a schedule worth knowing: GSA posts the new fiscal year's per diem files in mid-August, effective October 1. The POV mileage bulletin lands in late December, effective January 1, with occasional midyear changes (like the July 2026 increase). The State Dept foreign table and the DTMO non-foreign table both update on the 1st of every month. The check button asks the GSA API whether the next fiscal year's rates are live yet (needs the API key).

Per diem by day computed from legs

Lodging is per night, so the last day of the trip carries no lodging by default. Underway days start with all meals ticked as provided aboard. Edit any day; edits survive leg and rate changes for the same date and mode.

Set a start date and at least one leg with days to build the grid.

Reimbursable expenses

Everything outside per diem: airfare, ground transportation, POV mileage, parking and tolls, lodging taxes (not covered by the CONUS lodging rate), and communications. POV rows compute miles × rate; the mileage rate defaults from your trip start date per the GSA/IRS calendar-year rate and stays editable.

Itemizing. The ☰ button turns a line into a group: one \u201cUber/Lyft\u201d line with each trip as an entry underneath, summed automatically and itemized on the attachment and spreadsheet. Works for anything repetitive: garages under Parking & Tolls, wifi day passes under Communications. Prefix entries with the date for a clean audit trail. The bill period applies to the whole line, so on a trip you invoice monthly, keep one \u201cUber (Oct)\u201d line and one \u201cUber (Nov)\u201d line.

Receipts stay in this tab, never uploaded

Attach receipt images to an expense line with the 📎 button, or add lodging folios and other general receipts here. They print as labelled pages at the end of the expense attachment, scoped to the billing period.

Airfare benchmarks. There is no reliable free average-airfare feed this tool can query, so airfare stays a manual entry. For a defensible government benchmark, check the GSA City Pair Program fares (unrestricted YCA fares make a conservative estimate) or use your booking quote. Multi-leg itineraries can carry one airfare line per ticket.

Summary

Printable document

Two flavours of the same numbers. Expense report is backup detail for one line on your QuickBooks invoice: it references the invoice number and trip ID, itemizes the billing period, and appends receipts. Estimate is the before-travel version to send a client for approval: same detail, estimate framing, no invoice reference required.

Report branding printed document only

The printed estimate or expense report can carry your own letterhead: logo, heading colour, and the shaded band. The app itself and the attribution line stay as they are. Custom branding is remembered on this device.

Keep the band light; the text on it stays dark. Wide logos work best (the header gives them about 230px).
